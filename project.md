# Net Profit

A cosy isometric fishing tycoon for the browser. Tow a net through schools of fish, haul the catch home, build up the boat, build up the island, sail further, find bigger things.

This document hands the project from a chat-built prototype to a real repo. It describes what exists, how it works, what is wrong with it, and what comes next. The prototype is `legacy/net-profit.html` (one file, ~1,300 lines, no dependencies, no build). Treat that file as the behavioural reference until the port reaches parity. `index.html` at the repo root began as a copy of it and is where the port happens; the README covers how to run and test.

## Who this is for

The owner (engineer, final say) and her kid (co-designer, chief playtester, source of the best ideas in this doc). It started as "like Harvest Hero, but not wheat" and turned into something they both want to be real.

Working style the owner expects from a collaborator: real opinions, pushback when something is a bad idea, corrections over comfort. Say "I think this is wrong because…" and then do the work. No performing.

## Design pillars

1. **The sweep is the game.** The net is towed like a trailer, so turning swings it wide. Carving an arc through a school has to feel good before anything else matters. Never trade this away for features.
2. **Short loop, long horizon.** A trip is under a minute: catch, fill, dock, sell, buy. The horizon is the palace, the flagship, the leviathan, the next island.
3. **Base grows the boat, boat grows the world.** (Kid.) Building the island unlocks upgrade levels. Upgrades grow the boat. A bigger boat has a bigger range. Further out there are richer fish and stranger things.
4. **The game grows up with the player.** (Owner.) Each new island introduces a new verb and a visible step up in graphics and mechanical depth. Island 1 is flat shapes and trawling. Later islands earn shaders, weather, crew, line fishing. The game gets a bit meta about its own evolution.
5. **Cosy with teeth.** Threats exist (pirates, sharks, later ninjas) but always have a counter the player can buy, befriend, or outrun. Losing fish stings; nothing is ever lost permanently.
6. **Rare things sparkle.** (Kid.) Dawn and dusk ultra-rares, night-only glowing fish, a leviathan you see long before you can do anything about it. Discovery is a reward in itself; the catch log is a collection.
7. **Phone first.** One thumb, floating joystick, portrait. Everything is tested at 390×780. Desktop (WASD/arrows) is a bonus.

## What exists today

All of this is implemented and smoke-tested in the prototype.

**Core loop**
- Floating joystick (touch or mouse) that points where to go. The keyboard has two feels, chosen by a HUD button that appears where a mouse lives or once a key is pressed, and saved: drive (A and D turn the hull, W is throttle, S brakes; the default) or point (WASD and the arrows aim the boat like the stick, eased over 150 ms). Arrows match WASD in both. Boat has heading, turn rate, acceleration, drag.
- Net follows the stern at a fixed tow length (trailer physics). Catches any fish within `netWidth/2 + 5` of the net centre while the net is moving (> 22 u/s), the hold has room, and the net is not torn.
- Hold fills; HUD bar turns red and nudges when full; gold edge arrow points to the dock.
- Inside any room (tower floors and roofs, the hall and the demon dimension, the drowned temple, Gigantis) the boat's bubbles are put away: the fish order, the hold, the hull, the fishmonger's pick and the driftwood (body.inside). Coins, hearts and the time of day stay, and the message drops down less far over the fight. Everything comes back on the way out. At sea, in a boss fight too, they all stay: there the hull and the hold are what is at stake (2026-10-10, the owner's ask).
- Dock ring: entering it opens the shop and auto-sells one fish every 45 ms, cheapest first. Docking also mends a torn net. The shop covers where the thumb lives, so a drag on its wood steers the boat straight through it and the panel fades to a quarter while you do; buttons and the catch log still take taps (2026-09-17, after a playtester had to reach over the panel to leave).
- Upgrades: net width, hold size, engine speed. Six levels each.
- Ashore (2026-10-06), the kid's third stop: while docked, a Step ashore tab stands up off the shop's plank sign. Tap it and the boat comes round to its berth, bow in a hand's width off the end of the pier, over at most 1.6 s; then a little figure in a yellow sou'wester and a shirt in the hull's paint hops onto the planks and the shop folds away. The same stick walks it at 85 a second, the same pace in every direction, and full pace takes only half a push (added 2026-10-08: the owner found the monkeys too fast, because a thumb resting on a phone pushes about half way and walked at half pace, slower than a monkey; the tests had always pushed the stick all the way); the keys always point while ashore, since driving a person by turning them reads as a tank. Dry land is the island (to 12 short of the waterline, because on the far side the sand is squashed to half its depth on screen and a figure nearer the edge than its shadow looks as if it is wading), the pier but the strip behind the crates, the bridge deck between the rails and the beach. The hut, the palace tree and its ladder, the palms, the watchtower, the stall, the smokehouse, the crates, the umbrella and the toll sign stand in the way, and the figure slides along what it walks into. It steps up onto the pier and the bridge rather than popping, leaves footprints in the sand that fade over 7 s, and carries a lantern at night. The camera comes in 60% closer and follows it, and an edge marker points back to the boat. Back at the end of the pier, after a walk (not straight after landing, where the button would sit under the thumb that lands to start walking), a Back aboard button hops it onto the bow and the shop comes back. Nothing to do ashore yet, on purpose: walking has to feel right on a phone first. Being ashore is not saved; a trip saved ashore comes back aboard at the berth.

## How the prototype is built

One IIFE, plain canvas 2D, DOM for HUD and shop, Web Audio oscillators for sound, Grandstander from Google Fonts.

**Projection.** `K = √½`. Screen x = `((wx−camX) − (wy−camY))·K·Z + W/2`. Screen y = `((wx−camX) + (wy−camY))·K/2·Z + H/2 + viewDY − wz·Z`. A world circle of radius r draws as an ellipse `r·Z` by `r·Z/2`. Joystick screen vectors are inverted back to world with `a = dx/K, b = 2dy/K → ((a+b)/2, (b−a)/2)`. `Z` is a zoom derived from `min(W,H)/500`, clamped 0.62–1.35, eased by tier and by the dock view.

**Solids.** `extrude(poly, z0, z1, side, top)` draws only viewer-facing side quads (normal·(1,1) > 0), shaded by normal, then the top face. Ships are hull polygon + inset deck + cabin boxes rotated by heading, so they foreshorten correctly. Depth sorting is by `wx + wy` for a short list of drawables (hut, palms, tree, tower, boat, pirate). The pier is a special case that sorts relative to the boat's side of it.

**Update order.** clock → input → boat physics → island/pier push-out → world clamp → range limit → net tow → schools + catching → dock + selling → rare → leviathan → sparks → pirate → gulls + dolphins → sharks → salvage → driftwood → zoom → wakes/particles/texts → camera.

**Draw order.** sea (abyss, lagoon diamond, depth rings, glints, range ring, island flats, dock ring) → leviathan → fish → rare → sharks → dolphins → wakes → net → buoys → salvage → driftwood → depth-sorted objects → birds → flying-fish particles → night mask → glow pass (bioluminescence, sparkles) → floating text → edge indicators → joystick.

**Night.** A half-resolution offscreen canvas is filled with the ambient colour, lights are punched out with `destination-out` radial gradients scaled by `dark`, and the result is multiplied onto the scene. Glow species then get a `screen` halo and per-fish bright dots. This replaced an additive glow that washed the boat out; do not go back to adding light on top of sprites.

**Steering around land.** `around(x, y, tx, ty, R)` returns a tangent waypoint when the straight line to a target would cross the island. Dolphins and the pirate use it, with `pushOut` as a backstop. Any new roaming entity needs the same.

**Debug hook.** `window.__np` exposes boat, net, schools, pirate, sharks, pods, rare, lev, and setters for clock, wood and build; `hold` and `coins` are getters, `wood` is a setter only, so read the wood count from the HUD pill `#wood`. The smoke test drives the game through it, and so do balance probes in headless Chromium (max the levels, set `build` and `wood`, drop a log on the boat, read the shop). Keep an equivalent in dev builds.

## Known debt (fix during the port, not before)

- Global mutable state in one closure. No entity interface, no layers, no event bus.
- Species are coupled by array index (`SHARK = 7`, night = 8–9, rares = 10–11, `RING_R` parallel array). Move to string ids and data files; migrate the save's `log[]` to a map.
- `refreshShop()` rebuilds paint swatches and log chips via `innerHTML` on every sale tick.
- Toasts (fixed 2026-09-17): `ui/toast.ts` is a queue with three priorities; danger interrupts, the rest wait up to four seconds and give up, and a message never repeats back to back. Pirate and shark warnings are danger; dolphins, tier, palace, the leviathan and the range edge are events; everything else is routine.
- Pier depth-sort hack. Tall palace pieces can overlap the hut at some camera positions.
- Fish heading is derived from frame-to-frame position delta; schools that come on screen snap for a frame.
- Leviathan path speed is non-uniform (squircle parameterisation).
- No pause beyond clamping dt to 50 ms. The trip survives a discarded tab (see Persistence); entities do not, so schools, sharks and the pirate reset on reload.
- Sound is raw oscillators by design. `audio/sfx.ts` (2026-09-17) names the twenty-five cues and locks each to the legacy numbers by test; the catch, sell, driftwood blip and dolphin whistle are keepers (owner and kid, 2026-09-16 and 17). Music and ambience are separate layers and never replace a cue.
- Dead code: `isDark`, `mq` (night used to follow the OS theme).
- The palace is small. It reads as a treehouse wearing a hat. It deserves its own art pass.
- Accessibility: canvas has a label and the shop is real buttons, but there is no reduced-motion path for screen shake or the day-cycle tint.

- `npm run dev` serves the game at http://localhost:4830/ with hot reload; `npm run preview` serves the production build on 4831. Both ports are pinned strictly because other projects on the same machine use Vite's defaults. Never fall back to 5173 or 4173.
- Phone testing: `npm run dev -- --host`, then open the Network URL Vite prints. A tailnet hostname works too, since `*.ts.net` is allowed through Vite's host check. Reaching the dev server over the LAN on Windows needs an inbound firewall rule scoped to TCP 4830 on the private profile, not a blanket rule for node.
- `npm run test:e2e` runs the smoke test in Chromium against the production build on its own server. `npx playwright test --project=msedge` uses the Edge already on Windows when the Chromium download will not complete.
- Balance probes beat playing to flagship: drive the game in headless Chromium through `window.__np`, exactly as the driftwood retune was checked.
- The tester panel, added 2026-10-11 because the owner could not get past the Skeleton Shark King and wanted to see what lies beyond him: open the game with `#tester` at the end of its link and a dashed Tester button appears at the bottom of the shop, on that device only (`localStorage["netprofit.tester"]`), until `#tester-off`. It lists the story's steps in order (`src/ui/tester.ts`); tapping one marks it and every step before it beaten, never takes a stage back, saves, and reloads, because companions, bosses and doors all follow the stages on load. Its extras add 10,000 coins, 100 driftwood, clear all the sea map's fog at once (added 2026-10-13, by popular demand: islands not yet found show on it unnamed), the Reaper's ice fishing kit, the Reaper's egg (or the egg ready to hatch at once), the otter, Neil and the naga, and mend the boat. The egg buttons leave a Reaper on land who was never beaten asleep by his nest, and another button brings one already beaten back there for a rematch (both kept in the save as reaperRematch; beaten again he goes, with his prize again), added 2026-10-13 when a game rebuilt with the tester after the kid's home-screen game was lost had his egg but no Reaper to fight. The extras sit at the bottom of the panel, where a toast would be hidden behind it, so each button says itself that it worked (Added ✓, green, for a moment), and one you already have says Have it (added 2026-10-12, when the owner could not tell an Add had done anything, and found the otter and Neil missing). It is a real save change: a skipped game is that game from then on. A skip never skips a cutscene (added 2026-10-13, the kid: "cutscenes are important"): island 3's step leaves it at stage 3, so the tar monster's scene plays the next time the boat is out and moves it on to 4; and a skip past the Old One owes his rising (oldOwed in the save), which plays in full, soul armour and all, the next time the figure goes into the ruins, ending with him sinking back into the pool ("you beat the Old One long ago") instead of the fight, with no prize. The egg's hatching already plays from its extras.
- Play on another device, added 2026-10-11 because the owner's dad wanted his phone game on his laptop. There are no accounts, so the game travels in a link: the shop's "Play on another device" packs the save into `#game=…` (`src/state/share.ts`: deflate-raw through `CompressionStream` and base64url, a code starting `z`; plain base64url starting `j` where a browser cannot compress). A late-game save makes a link of about 1,200 characters. Opened, the link shows both games (day, coins, the last boss past) and asks before it replaces the game on that device; loading stops the old game saving over it as the page goes. The panel also takes a link pasted in, because an iPhone's home-screen game keeps its own storage apart from Safari's and opens no links; `#tester` pasted there works too. The `#…` is read on load and on `hashchange`, and tidied from the address bar once read.
- Online saves, added 2026-10-16, the owner's call: anyone who finds the game, so no accounts and nothing personal (the save holds nothing a player types), and a save code, with the free plan's pausing accepted. Each game has a save code, four words from 256 short, easy-to-spell ones from the game's world (`src/state/cloud.ts`, about four billion codes), made once and kept in the save (`saveCode`); a game from before codes makes one on its first load. The game puts itself online under it, squeezed as for a link: a game already here at once on load, then at most once a minute while it is played (every save hands it on), and as the page goes. The other end is in the owner's waterways Supabase project (her pick: the free plan runs two projects, and waterways already runs), kept apart from that app: a table `net_profit.saves` (code, game, earned, when) in a schema the API does not serve, with row security on and no policies, and two functions, `public.np_save_game` and `public.np_load_game`, the only way in (`supabase/migrations/net_profit_saves.sql`, applied there as the migration net_profit_saves). The table refuses a game that has earned less than the one it holds, so an old copy on another device cannot write over a newer one; refused, the game offers the newer one (Played on further on another device), once, and asks before it loads it. The Play on another device card shows the code first, with Copy and how the copy online is doing (saved just now, minutes ago; played on further elsewhere; cannot be reached, and it keeps trying), then the link as before, which needs nothing online; its box takes a code, typed any way (case, spaces, dashes), or a link, and what comes of it is said under the box (a toast would be behind the card). Once a week, the kid's yes to a reminder, starting the game toasts its code to write down (`codeTold`); a new game waits its first week, a game from before codes hears it at once. Offline, or with the project asleep, nothing changes but that the copy online waits. The page's key is the project's publishable key, public by design. The database's security advisor flags the two functions as callable without signing in (meant: they are the only door) and the table as having row security but no policies (meant: nobody reads it directly). A game is at most 64 KB online (a real one is a few). The smoke tests never reach the project: each is cut off from it, and the one about it answers for it.
- Installable, added 2026-10-11 because the owner wanted the game as an app on her phone: `public/manifest.webmanifest` (standalone, with the 192, 512 and maskable 512 icons), the iOS home-screen meta tags (full screen, under the status bar, which the safe-area insets already allow for), and `public/sw.js`, registered in production builds only. The service worker fetches the page from the network first, so a deploy arrives on the next open, and falls back to its kept copy with no signal; the hashed scripts come from the cache, and each fresh page prunes the ones it no longer uses. Lookups ignore `Vary`, because `vite preview` varies by Origin and a module script request would otherwise miss. It never touches saves. On an iPhone the home-screen app keeps its own storage apart from Safari's: carry a game across with "Play on another device". Updates (2026-10-12, after the owner asked whether the home-screen app would pick up new versions): a phone pauses the app in the background and brings back the same page, so coming back to the front, and every quarter of an hour while visible, the game fetches its page with `cache: 'no-store'` (which the worker passes straight to the network) and compares the hashed script it names with the running one (`src/state/update.ts`). Newer: on the title screen it reloads at once; mid-trip a toast says so and the shop offers "A new version is ready" at the next dock, saving before it reloads, so nothing at sea is cut short. The worker revalidates the page with the server on every navigation (`cache: 'no-cache'`), so a page held a few minutes in the browser's own cache after a deploy cannot bring the old game back.

Run `node scripts/icons.mjs` after changing the picture (`CHROMIUM=` points it at a Chromium).

## Roadmap (ideas discussed; the built ones say so)

Near:
- **Ninjas.** The night threat, opposite of pirates: silent, empty your crab pots, never seen. Later hireable as palace guards and sushi chefs. Possible late-game faction choice, pirates vs ninjas.
- **Crab pots.** Drop, leave, return. Passive income and route planning. The thing ninjas rob.
- **Line fishing for legendaries.** Second verb: one giant shadow per zone, timing minigame, trophy mounted on the palace. Nets are volume; lines are single targets. The first legendary is the snook, below.
- **Ambience.** Built 2026-09-17 as designed here; first tuned 2026-09-18, when the owner heard the pad as an engine hum on desktop speakers and no waves at all: a phone speaker cannot play 87 Hz, so the first mix had only ever been heard without its pad, which on full-range speakers masked the swell. The pad went up an octave and down by half, the swell's cutoff up, and the surf came in. Check every sound change on both. Procedural, on its own bus that ducks 40% under every cue for half a second so nothing cute gets stepped on. Waves: brown noise low-passed near 700 Hz on a slow wandering swell, a surf layer (a band-pass sweeping 650 to 1,100 Hz under two low-pass lids at 1,250 Hz, cresting with each swell, always on, which is what reads as water on any speaker; measured offline it leads the mix at a crest with the whistle's band 17 dB beneath it), two LFO phases for stereo, a brighter hiss that rises with boat speed, a quick lap inside the dock ring, lower and quieter at night. Gulls: a triangle sliding 1.1 kHz to 700 Hz with a fast tremolo, one to three syllables, only from birds on screen and awake, one cry every five to twelve seconds, silent for two seconds after an escort starts. The calm layer: three detuned triangles on F3, C4 and F4 under a slow chorus, low-passed at 800 Hz, an A4 at dawn and dusk, two notes at night; rooted on F because the dolphin whistle ends on C7 and D7. Wind chimes near shore (owner, 2026-09-17): sparse F-pentatonic pings with long decays and a couple of bright partials, fading in within about 400 units of the island and the pier, panned toward it, never more than one every few seconds. Dolphin chirps (kid, 2026-09-17): soft single chirps, quieter than the whistle, from a pod within a few hundred units every several seconds with jitter, panned toward it, an occasional double while escorting, and none within two seconds of the escort whistle. Everything stays out of the whistle's 1.4 to 2.4 kHz band.
- **Palace art pass.** Wings, rope bridge to the hut, dock gate, lights. Make stage 5 worth 258 driftwood.
- **Leviathan as a gate.** Started 2026-09-28: it guards the buoys (under Creatures). It should become the thing between you and the next island once there is one past the deep.
- **Stages 4 and 5 should unlock something.** Done 2026-09-18: the watchtower opens the fishmonger and the dome the smokehouse (Vendors, under What exists today). Boat-side rewards for the two stages are still open.
- **Background music.** A layer under the sfx with its own volume, sharing the mute toggle. Tracks load from `assets/music/` and the game stays silent when the folder is empty, so CI and Pages work without it. The folder is gitignored on purpose: the placeholder tracks there are a commercial soundtrack and cannot ship. Needs licensed or original music before it goes live.

Creatures (brainstormed 2026-09-17, in the order they should be built):

- **Jellyfish pools.** Built 2026-09-18 as entities/jellies.ts. One bloom of twenty to forty jellies drifts at 14 a second, its heading wandering, keeping 240 off the shore ring and 300 off the rim; the jellies pulse, the bloom is lit in the night mask and glows through the glow pass. A moving, whole net that passes within half its width plus 6 of a jelly scoops it: the first scoop toasts and squelches, the school sweep and the rare catch stop while any are in the net, the net shows them as pale blobs, and docking shakes them out with a count and three quick shakes. Scooped jellies regrow after a minute. Cosy with teeth. The shipwright sells the fine mesh that lets a net pass clean (2026-09-18).
- **Manta rays, for the owner.** Built 2026-09-18 as entities/mantas.ts. A squadron of three to five glides the middle rings, 1,200 to 1,700 out, at 46 a second, clockwise, the other way round from the whales, in a loose V behind the biggest. Each is a kite with beating wingtips and a whip of a tail, drawn as a shadow under the water. With the boat within 750 of the leader, every 14 to 30 seconds one of them leaps: it rises 78 off its shadow over 1.7 seconds, shows a pale belly as it banks, and comes down in a ring of spray with a low whump (heard within 900, with an octave on top so a phone can play it). The net slides off them. The boat within 420 is a sighting: a toast the first time, a gold chip, a page in the guide, and mantaSeen in the save. Purely spectacle: the kind of thing you point at when someone else is holding the phone.
- **Whales, for the kid.** Built 2026-09-18 as entities/whales.ts and a voice in the ambience. A mother (58 long) and her calf cruise round the island between 1,750 and 2,250 out at 24 a second, the calf on station behind and beside her. They travel as shadows and come up to breathe for four and a half seconds with a spout and a slate back, the mother every 11 to 18 seconds and the calf more often. The net slides over them; nothing catches or harms them. The boat within 450 of the mother is a sighting: a toast the first time, a gold chip in the log and a page in the guide. They sing when in earshot, 1,400 by day and 2,300 at night: the mother a phrase of two to four glides between 110 and 420 Hz every 14 to 26 seconds, the calf answering a second or two later between 260 and 620 Hz, into a shared echo. The roadmap's 80 to 400 Hz would have been silent on a phone, so each voice carries overtones a phone can play, and every partial stays under the dolphin whistle's 1.4 kHz; a test holds them to it.
- **Snook, for the owner's dad.** Built 2026-09-19, his birthday. The first fish taken on a line, in fishing/snook.ts. Silver, a black line down its side, yellow fins. It holds in the shadow of the Ten Cent Bridge's far abutment at dusk, night and dawn; a net towed over it finds nothing but a splash. Tie up within 170 of the spot on the south side, all but stopped, and a Cast button appears: ten coins a cast, which is how the bridge earns its name. The boat holds still and the thumb works the rod: hold to pull when it sulks, let go when it runs. Any press pulls in full, however far it drags, and so does any held key. Pull through a run and the line parts; pull too little and it reaches the piling. Before every run but the first it shakes its head for 0.45 seconds, throwing spray, with three ticks of the reel, and the word over the tension bar turns from Hold to Let go; it still pulls like a sulk while it does. That tell was added 2026-09-24, when the owner reported the snook could not be caught, and she was right: the fight had only been played by a test hand that eased off on the very frame a run began. A thumb a third of a second late, through the game's stick smoothing, pulled through the start of every run, the line ratcheted tighter each cycle, and it parted before the fish tired. It landed one short in two hundred and no giants; in the real game a hand that late lost five landable fish of five to the line. That was not enough. The owner still could not catch it, and two more things were wrong. The pull was how far the stick was dragged, and a thumb that dragged under two thirds of the ring lost every fish to the piling; a natural hold on a phone is a short drag. And a hand that did exactly what the words said, holding on a sulk and letting go for a run, lost most shorts to the piling anyway, because a run took back about as much line as a whole sulk of pulling won (the test hand had only won by keeping a little pressure on through each run, which a thumb on glass cannot do). So any press is a full pull, and the reel pulls at 48 (was 34), half again as fast as a run, so a sulk wins back more than a run takes. In the real game, a thumb dragging 15 px a third of a second late, doing what the words said, lost five landable fish of five to the piling before and landed five of five after. The hooked fish is drawn on the line from the abutment to the boat, as far out as it has been let run, not at a fixed spot south of the abutment, where it sat under the hull for most of the fight. The honest part, and the owner's numbers: every cast decides before the strike what is on the line. One in ten is a snook that can be landed, and most of those are shorts (18 to 27 inches) that go back; one in fifty is inside the slot (28 to 32) and big enough to keep, and goes up on the hut's wall; one keeper in five is the giant, Grampy's Snook (40 to 46), over the slot, which goes back after the photo and hangs as a gilt frame beside the mount, an Easter egg with its own guide page once landed. On every other cast the snook makes a last run no rod could stop. The tests play it with a person who does only what the words say: the thumb is down or up, everything reaches it a third of a second late, give or take a tenth, and the rod follows it as it does in the game, with no partial pressure and no saving a fish from the piling. That hand lands every landable short and keeper and about four giants in five; half a second late it lands the same, since the giant gets away by stamina, not by reflexes; the same hand that waits for the run instead of the tell lands under one short in ten, so reading the tell is the skill. The giant runs at 36 (was 34) so that it stays the one that gets away; this entry used to promise five in six. A heavy hand always parts the line and a timid one always loses it to the piling; a test of twenty thousand casts by that person holds the odds. Every miss ends the same way: "The snook is still under the bridge." The words over the fish say which miss it was: "The line parted", "Into the piling", or, on the last run no rod could stop, "Never coming in". Those last words were added 2026-09-26: the owner kept losing to the piling and could not tell her own misses from the nine casts in ten nobody lands, which all reach the piling on the third run, about six seconds after the strike. The bridge is named for one the owner's family crossed to reach the beach.

## Things learned the hard way

- A canvas with `position:fixed; inset:0` does not stretch. Set `width:100%; height:100%` or the backing store draws at the wrong scale.
- Additive glow over sprites at night washes them out and makes them look darker. Use a darkness mask with holes.
- Panning the camera the moment the boat crosses the dock ring feels like being yanked. Wait until the boat has slowed, zoom out, drift gently.
- Anything that roams needs to know the island exists.
- In night scenes, anything the player must find (driftwood, crates, rares) needs its own small light.
- Test at phone size first. The isometric squash halves vertical distances, so the world feels smaller on screen than the numbers suggest.
- Vite's default ports are shared by every Vite project on the machine, and Playwright's `reuseExistingServer` will happily test whichever app answers first. Pin ports and never reuse.
- Vite blocks any Host header that is not localhost or an IP address. A dev server reached by hostname needs `server.allowedHosts`.
- A network that advertises IPv6 without routing it hangs Node downloads, including Playwright's browser installer, while curl falls back to IPv4 in milliseconds. If curl works and Node does not, suspect IPv6 first.
- Git Bash's tar cannot read zip files. `C:\Windows\System32\tar.exe` can.
- A force-push does not start a GitHub Pages branch build, and with the Pages source set to GitHub Actions nothing deploys until a workflow exists on `main`.
- Phone browsers discard a backgrounded tab. Anything not saved is a lost trip.
- A panel that covers the thumb's home must let steering through. Pointer capture makes it one binding: a press on the panel's background anchors the stick and hands the pointer to the canvas.
- iOS ignores `user-scalable=no`, so a double-tap on any button zooms the page, and once zoomed the canvas's `touch-action: none` swallows the pinch that would zoom back out. `touch-action: manipulation` on the body kills double-tap zoom everywhere and leaves pinch alone. Found by the chief playtester buying an upgrade twice, fast.
- Coins come from the sweep and wood from errands, so a base gated only on wood always trails the boat. Fix supply before price, and give the late stages something to gate.
- Price nothing off a single big docking. Take two screenshots ten minutes apart of the owner actually playing and divide; that number was a tenth of the burst.
- A timing test played by a hand that reacts on the same frame proves nothing about people. Give the test hand a person's reaction, a third of a second, and the game's own input smoothing. The snook's fight passed a perfect hand and parted every line a person played.
- Then give it a person's input too. A thumb on glass is down or up, and a short drag is how people hold. The snook's second fix passed a test hand that dragged three quarters of the ring and eased to a fifth through every run; the owner, holding the way people hold, lost every fish to the piling.
- Trigger a threat on what the player did, not where they are pointing. The leviathan's guard first rose for any flagship heading at the buoys, and the lanternfish school 280 in from them: it would have woken on every sweep of the richest fish in the game.
- Players reach for what the world shows them. The day they could walk past the dog, both players went looking for a way to pet it. Make the obvious touch work, and show how until it is found.
- On the far side of an isometric island a figure stands in front of the water behind it, so one walked to the waterline looks as if it is wading. Keep feet a shadow's width inside the sand.
- Eight-way keys on an isometric map point at a diagonal the boat is rarely facing, so the throttle penalty for turning is on almost all the time and the keyboard feels slower and twitchier than the stick. Put feels side by side behind a switch and let the players pick. Two players picked two different ones, and a setting was cheaper than a winner.
- A grey screen on a phone is the page's process killed, not a script error, which leaves the last frame frozen on the screen. Look for what grows over a long session.
- Safari keeps any Web Audio node that is still connected, and runs it on every block of sound, for good. Every note must disconnect what it made when it ends (`onended`). Before that fix (2026-10-12, #157) ten minutes of gulls, dolphins and whales left 2,727 nodes running, the likely cause of the owner's grey screen during a Star encounter. `tests/unit/audionodes.test.ts` plays the cues and the sea through a fake engine and checks every note unplugs.
- Playwright cannot route a fetch that a service worker handles. A test that serves the page something else runs with `serviceWorkers: 'block'`, and what the worker does is tested on its own (offline, in the installable test).
- A global `canvas{position:fixed;inset:0}` pins every canvas to the top left. A second canvas, as the radar is, needs `top:auto;left:auto`.
- A new game starts just off the dock ring, not in it. A test that means "docked" puts the boat on `DOCK`.
