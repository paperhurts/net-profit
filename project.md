# Net Profit

A cosy isometric fishing tycoon for the browser. Tow a net through schools of fish, haul the catch home, build up the boat, build up the island, sail further, find bigger things.

This document hands the project from a chat-built prototype to a real repo. It describes what exists, how it works, what is wrong with it, and what comes next. The prototype is `legacy/net-profit.html` (one file, ~1,300 lines, no dependencies, no build). Treat that file as the behavioural reference until the port reaches parity. `index.html` at the repo root began as a copy of it and is where the port happens; the README covers how to run and test.

## Who this is for

The owner (engineer, final say) and her kid (co-designer, chief playtester, source of the best ideas in this doc). It started as "like Harvest Hero, but not wheat" and turned into something they both want to be real.

Working style the owner expects from a collaborator: real opinions, pushback when something is a bad idea, corrections over comfort. Say "I think this is wrong because…" and then do the work. No performing.

## Design pillars

1. **The sweep is the game.** The net is towed like a trailer, so turning swings it wide. Carving an arc through a school has to feel good before anything else matters. Never trade this away for features.
2. **Short loop, long horizon.** A trip is under a minute: catch, fill, dock, sell, buy. The horizon is the palace, the flagship, the leviathan, the next island.
3. **Base grows the boat, boat grows the world.** (Kid.) Building the island unlocks upgrade levels. Upgrades grow the boat. A bigger boat has a bigger range. Further out there are richer fish and stranger things.
4. **The game grows up with the player.** (Owner.) Each new island introduces a new verb and a visible step up in graphics and mechanical depth. Island 1 is flat shapes and trawling. Later islands earn shaders, weather, crew, line fishing. The game gets a bit meta about its own evolution.
5. **Cosy with teeth.** Threats exist (pirates, sharks, later ninjas) but always have a counter the player can buy, befriend, or outrun. Losing fish stings; nothing is ever lost permanently.
6. **Rare things sparkle.** (Kid.) Dawn and dusk ultra-rares, night-only glowing fish, a leviathan you see long before you can do anything about it. Discovery is a reward in itself; the catch log is a collection.
7. **Phone first.** One thumb, floating joystick, portrait. Everything is tested at 390×780. Desktop (WASD/arrows) is a bonus.

## What exists today

All of this is implemented and smoke-tested in the prototype.

**Core loop**
- Floating joystick (touch or mouse) that points where to go. The keyboard has two feels, chosen by a HUD button that appears where a mouse lives or once a key is pressed, and saved: drive (A and D turn the hull, W is throttle, S brakes; the default) or point (WASD and the arrows aim the boat like the stick, eased over 150 ms). Arrows match WASD in both. Boat has heading, turn rate, acceleration, drag.
- Net follows the stern at a fixed tow length (trailer physics). Catches any fish within `netWidth/2 + 5` of the net centre while the net is moving (> 22 u/s), the hold has room, and the net is not torn.
- Hold fills; HUD bar turns red and nudges when full; gold edge arrow points to the dock.
- Dock ring: entering it opens the shop and auto-sells one fish every 45 ms, cheapest first. Docking also mends a torn net. The shop covers where the thumb lives, so a drag on its wood steers the boat straight through it and the panel fades to a quarter while you do; buttons and the catch log still take taps (2026-09-17, after a playtester had to reach over the panel to leave).
- Upgrades: net width, hold size, engine speed. Six levels each.
- Ashore (2026-10-06), the kid's third stop: while docked, a Step ashore tab stands up off the shop's plank sign. Tap it and the boat comes round to its berth, bow in a hand's width off the end of the pier, over at most 1.6 s; then a little figure in a yellow sou'wester and a shirt in the hull's paint hops onto the planks and the shop folds away. The same stick walks it at 85 a second, the same pace in every direction, and full pace takes only half a push (added 2026-10-08: the owner found the monkeys too fast, because a thumb resting on a phone pushes about half way and walked at half pace, slower than a monkey; the tests had always pushed the stick all the way); the keys always point while ashore, since driving a person by turning them reads as a tank. Dry land is the island (to 12 short of the waterline, because on the far side the sand is squashed to half its depth on screen and a figure nearer the edge than its shadow looks as if it is wading), the pier but the strip behind the crates, the bridge deck between the rails and the beach. The hut, the palace tree and its ladder, the palms, the watchtower, the stall, the smokehouse, the crates, the umbrella and the toll sign stand in the way, and the figure slides along what it walks into. It steps up onto the pier and the bridge rather than popping, leaves footprints in the sand that fade over 7 s, and carries a lantern at night. The camera comes in 60% closer and follows it, and an edge marker points back to the boat. Back at the end of the pier, after a walk (not straight after landing, where the button would sit under the thumb that lands to start walking), a Back aboard button hops it onto the bow and the shop comes back. Nothing to do ashore yet, on purpose: walking has to feel right on a phone first. Being ashore is not saved; a trip saved ashore comes back aboard at the berth.

- The Skeleton Shark King (2026-10-08), the kid's island 5 boss, "like Jaws on the NES": a great shark of bare bones, half again the boat's length, with a gold crown on his skull and a gold trident, fought from the boat with the harpoon. The trident: he swims out to 220 to 420 from the boat, rises, and raises it for 1.1 s (the harpoon reaches him then too); a gold ring shows where the boat will be for the last half second, and the trident lands 0.7 s later (20). Ten harpoon hits, shown as pips; past half he is angry, with a red eye, 0.7 s of churn and less stalking between. Sail 1,400 from the reef, or be eaten (spat out at home as for any boat eaten), and he sinks back, whole. Harpooned down, he rolls over at the surface and dies, dramatically (the kid's): letterbox bars, the camera on him, the markers away, and in a speech bubble "Argh... I know of a portal... a portal to another world...", "It lies on the island of... of...", "...", then his eye goes out to an X and the narration says he is dead (12 s; a tap skips it). 3,000 coins, his crown left on his throne, a log chip and a guide page; saved as isle5Stage 1, and he stays dead. In the tests a person who sails round the reef, makes for him whenever he is up, turns off the line a third of a second after the churn and steers out of rings wins every time in about a minute at almost no cost; one who takes 0.6 s loses about a fifth of the boat; one who never minds the warnings loses most of it and is sometimes eaten.
- Island 2 (2026-10-07), the first island past the leviathans: in the far corner of the deep opposite the Cthuluviathan's city, 600 past both lines of buoys, straight across the screen from home and 4,200 from it, so only the flagship reaches it. Its dock ring faces home; entering it sells the hold as at home (the fish fly to the trading post) and opens the shop, and the dock camera frames island 2. Step ashore and the boat runs its bow up onto the sand and the figure walks the island; the dog stays home. Three schools of parrotfish ring it, 400 to 460 from its centre, built from their own seed. While the flagship is past the buoys a green marker at the screen's edge points to its dock, pulsing until it is found; the first time the boat comes within 700 of its shore it is announced and logged (isle2Seen). The anglerfish keeps 450 off its shore.
- 36 schools in rings by value. Fish respawn 8–17 s after being caught (plus 1.5 s per species index). Schools visibly thin.
- Ambience (2026-09-17): waves that breathe and hiss with speed, a calm pad rooted on F that warms at dawn and dusk and thins at night, wind chimes within 400 units of the shore or pier, gulls crying from birds that are around and awake, and dolphins chirping when a pod is within 350 units. All procedural on one bus that ducks 40% under every cue for half a second, behind the sound toggle. Levels are a first guess and want ears.
- Day cycle, 300 s: dawn (12%), day (43%), dusk (12%), night (33%). Night is a multiply mask with light-shaped holes (boat, net, hut, crates, treehouse, driftwood, salvage, glowing schools). Dawn and dusk tint warm.

**Creatures and hazards**
- Seven day species, two night-only glowing species, sharks, two sparkle rares. Table below.
- Gulls circle schools that still have fish (3 = full, 2 = thinning, 0 = fished out), trail the boat as the hold fills, sleep at night. Two perch on the island.
- Two dolphin pods roam. Pass one at speed and it escorts you for up to 30 s; while escorted, sharks will not charge.
- Six sharks orbit tuna, goldfin and lanternfish schools. They charge a moving net within 330 u. Net level < 4: net torn for 6 s, up to 3 fish spilled. Net level ≥ 4: the shark is caught (40 coins).
- One pirate, unlocked at 60 lifetime coins. Never follows a boat past the buoys: the deep is the leviathan's. Prowls, chases when you carry ≥ 4 fish, steals half the hold (highest species index first), leaves, returns in ~30–50 s. Never comes within ~400 u of the dock. Slightly faster than a level-1 engine.
- Manta rays: a squadron glides the middle rings in a V and, with the boat near, one leaps now and then and lands with a whump. The net slides off them. A sighting is logged.
- Whales: a mother and calf circle the far water, surface to breathe with a spout, and sing when in earshot (further at night). Not catchable. A sighting is logged.
- Leviathan: a 28-segment serpent shadow on a squircle path ~2,180 u out. Spines surface, spots glow at night. Passing within 300 u rumbles the screen and logs a sighting. Not catchable yet. It guards the buoys (2026-09-28): the moment a flagship crosses them, either way, it dives off its path and rises across the boat's way, 1.6 s of sailing ahead (380 to 700 u), spines first over 1.1 s with a growl, lit at night. It stays up while the boat keeps coming (up to 9 s) and sinks 2.4 s after the boat turns away or stops; then it will not rise again for 20 s, so the way is open. While it is up it is a wall: the first touch knocks a third of the hold overboard, most valuable first, shoves the boat back to its own side and stops it; after that it only holds the boat there. Never the boat, never coins.
- The Cthuluviathan (2026-10-06), the kid's leviathan, named by him: an octopus for a head, a fistful of tentacles for a face and two small wings, asleep in a sunken city of broken columns in one corner of the deep, 650 past both lines of buoys. Go slowly and it dreams on, snoring, with Zs rising. A boat within 750 going faster than 170 wakes it, and it stirs as a warning above 120; any boat within 360 wakes it. Awake, its eyes open yellow-green and every 0.9 s it sends a tentacle up where the boat will be in a second, within 1,000 of its city; the water boils there for that second first, so the counter is to steer off the bubbles. A tentacle that catches the boat takes a fifth of the hold, most valuable first, and slows the boat; never the boat, never coins; it cannot grab again for 1.5 s. Out of reach for 5 s, or awake for 30, and it goes back to sleep, soundly for 12 s. Within 1,800 its place is marked at the edge of the screen, a z while it sleeps and an eye awake, because a phone in portrait shows little to either side. In the tests a person steering off the bubbles a third of a second late is never caught, half a second late in one encounter in ten, and one who runs straight for open water without minding the bubbles about once an encounter. A sighting: a toast, a gold chip, a guide page and cthuluSeen in the save.
- The anglerfish (2026-10-06), out in the deep at night: it comes up once it is dark (0.5) and sinks at dawn (0.3), somewhere in the deep clear of the buoys, the deep's end and the Cthuluviathan's city, and drifts at 22 a second. Its lure is a bright bulb on a stalk with ten fish circling it drawn as lanternfish to the life; there are no glowing fish in the deep, which is the tell, with something large and dark beneath. It edges the light toward a boat within 1,000, no nearer than 300. A boat making for the light at more than 60, within 1.7 s of sailing (380 to 600), opens its jaws: a dark mouth ringed with teeth and the bulb flickering red, with a rising creak. They snap shut on the boat the moment it is over them (140 plus the hull), or on nothing if it turns away or 3 s pass. Caught, it swallows the boat whole (since 2026-10-07, the kid's: before, it tore the net and took a quarter of the hold), and the boat is spat out at home without its catch, as for any boat eaten (the Boat health entry). Either way it sinks and stays down 35 s, then comes up elsewhere, at least 1,300 from the boat. It is not marked at the screen's edge: it is a trap, and its light is what it shows you. In the tests a boat holding its course is always caught, and a person turning back or a quarter turn away up to two thirds of a second late never is (a probe caught some at eight tenths). A sighting the first time its jaws open: a toast, a gold chip, a guide page and anglerSeen in the save.
- The gulper (2026-10-07), the kid's leviathan that eats boats: a giant gulper eel, black with a mouth like a pelican's pouch and a pink light at the tip of its tail, which prowls the east side of the deep (180 in from the buoys and the deep's end, 500 short of each corner) deep down at 70 a second. A flagship within 900 in or near its water sets it hunting, with a groan and a toast and a red marker at the screen's edge: it surfaces, mouth widening as it closes, and chases at up to 290 (a flagship does 350) turning at 1.5 a second, slowing to turn so a boat inside its turning circle cannot keep it circling. A bite takes 34 of the boat's health and it lunges straight on for 0.9 s; three bites and it eats the boat. It gives up after 14 s, or when the boat crosses home or leaves its water by 600, and rests 20 s. In the tests a person running from it a third of a second late is bitten three times in two hundred hunts and never eaten; a boat that sits still is eaten nine times in ten. A sighting: a toast, a chip, a guide page and gulperSeen in the save.
- Boat health (2026-10-07), the kid's: 100, shown as a heart and a bar in the right-hand chips whenever the boat is past the buoys or not whole. It only goes down in the deep: the leviathan's hit takes 30, a tentacle 20 and a gulper bite 34, on top of the fish they take. At none, whatever did it swallows the boat: the view closes on it, stays dark a moment and opens again in the home shallows, where the boat is spat out whole with an empty hold and full health. **Progression**
- Boat tier = `floor((net + hold + engine levels) / 3)`, 0–5: dinghy, skiff, cutter, trawler, seiner, flagship. Each tier scales the hull, adds detail (wider cabin → second deck → stern crane), zooms the camera out 2%, unlocks paint, and raises the sailing range.
- Range per tier is a dashed ring; beyond it the boat is pushed back with a toast. The buoys hold every boat but the flagship, whose range runs on into the deep.
- Upgrade level cap = `min(5, 2 + buildStage)`. The base gates the boat.
- Driftwood (22 logs, 65% spawn inside current range, and respawn 18–40 s after pickup with the same bias) builds the treehouse palace in five stages. Each stage adds 15% to all sale prices, and stages 4 and 5 open the vendors below. The dock camera zooms out to frame the island once the boat settles.
- Orders: "12 mackerel pays 40". Filling one pays a bonus and rolls a new one, restricted to species inside your range. The gold edge arrow points to the nearest school of the order species.
- Vendors: the fishmonger (from the watchtower) picks a species at dawn (or on load, if he is open and has never asked) from what the boat can reach, leaning deeper like the orders, and pays double for it that day; the HUD chip and the sign on his stall show the pick. The smokehouse (from the palace dome) pays half again for tuna, goldfin and shark. Both stack with the palace bonus, and fish sold to a vendor fly to its building on the beach south of the pier.
- Pets: the dog comes with the tree platform, potters on the pier between the root and the crates, and when the pirate sails in it runs to the end of the planks and barks for four seconds: the early warning, with a toast at most once a minute. Its shape is a stand-in until the kid's drawing: a charcoal dog with a red collar and a pale muzzle, after the husband took the first, brown one for another box on the pier. Ashore (2026-10-07) it comes to say hello and follows at heel, 18 behind, along the way the figure actually walked, so nothing on the island can strand it; it sits by the figure facing it, barks where it stands if the pirate prowls, and goes back to its planks when the figure boards. A tap on it with the figure within 40 pets it: three hearts rise, it wags hard and yips. Both the owner and her kid went looking for a way to pet it the day they could walk past it, so until the first pet ever a faint heart floats over it while it sits, a toast says how, and the save remembers (petted).
- The shipwright: from cutter up, a Shipwright row in the shop sells one-off gear for coins (the Gear table has the prices): the fine mesh, which lets jellyfish slip through the net, and the strongbox, which holds the pirate to a quarter of the hold. It is the coin sink the endgame lacked, and each piece is the buyable counter to something with teeth. Once all three upgrades are maxed their cards fold away, so the panel is shorter with the shelf than it was without.
- The Ten Cent Bridge: named for a bridge the owner's family crossed to reach the beach. It leaves the island's west shore at 200 degrees, in the gap between two sardine schools, and runs 232 units on pilings and two stone abutments to a little beach with an umbrella and a towel. A painted sign at the beach end gives the toll (the palace tree hides the island end from the camera), and a lamp on the first abutment lights it at night. It is drawn in six parts along the span, each depth-sorted on its own, since one object that long cannot have one depth (sorted round the boat like the pier, it painted itself over the palace tree). Hulls are pushed off the span and the beach as they are off the pier; flotsam, the rare fish and the jellyfish keep clear of both. It is a place for now; the snook comes later.
- The snook: the first fish taken on a line, from a boat tied up south of the Ten Cent Bridge when the light is low, at ten coins a cast. One cast in ten lands one, one in fifty is big enough to keep and hangs on the hut, and the rest end with "The snook is still under the bridge." Hold to pull, let go to ease off: it shakes its head before every run, and a word over the tension bar says Hold or Let go (2026-09-24, after the owner found it could not be caught, twice). The one that was never coming in says so as it goes (2026-09-26). The roadmap entry has the whole of it.
- Salvage crates (10) give instant coins, scaled by tier.
- Catch log chips in the shop; rares get a gold ring; leviathan sighting is logged. Tap the chips or the Field guide card to open the guide: a page per species with where, when, worth, count and the day of the first catch. Days count dawns from 1.
- Ten hull paints; net floats and palace flags match the hull.
- Flags (2026-10-07), the kid's: a Your flag card beside the field guide in the shop opens a designer. Pick a colour (ten), a pattern (plain, stripe, cross, diagonal, halves, border), a second colour and an emblem (none, star, fish, skull, anchor, heart, crown); every tap flies it at once. It replaces the pennant on the boat's mast and on the palace and watchtower poles, and will go up on every tower taken. data/flag.ts turns the four numbers into shapes that both the canvas and the designer's SVG previews draw. Saved as flag; null in old saves, which keep the pennant in the hull's paint until a flag is designed.
- The spear (2026-10-07), the kid's, a fishing tool first: a third card on the shipwright's shelf sells it in levels, once island 2 has been found (before that a new player could spend 500 on a spear with nowhere to use it; the owner hit exactly that in a fresh game, so the card stays hidden until then, and the island 2 sighting says the spear is on sale), the fishing spear (500: reach 95, 0.9 s between throws), the long spear (1,500: 125, 0.7 s) and the barbed spear (3,500: 150, 0.55 s, hits twice as hard, for the monkeys). Ten parrotfish nose slowly round island 2 between 20 and 70 off its sand, coming back 25 s after they are taken. Ashore with a spear, a round Throw button appears at the right edge whenever one is in reach (Space on a keyboard; a tap on the fish works too), and the figure throws: the spear arcs out and always lands, following the fish as it swims, and since the boat is tied up at the trading post the fish is sold on the spot and flies to it. The figure carries the spear over its shoulder and raises its arm to throw. data/spear.ts holds the levels, entities/shallows.ts the fish and entities/spears.ts the throws; the save gained spear.
- The monkey camp (2026-10-07), the kid's evil monkeys in little skull masks: three thatched huts round a fire, a skull on a pole and a chest, on the far side of island 2 from its landing. Five monkeys mill about until the figure comes within 150 of the camp, then three run at it to bonk it and two keep a throw away and lob coconuts at where it stands, a shadow marking where each will land. All real time, never turn-based: they run at 60 and the figure walks at 85, so the fight is to keep moving and throw; they give up past 280 from the camp. Ashore on island 2 the figure has three hearts (shown top right); a bonk or a coconut takes one, knocks it back and leaves it blinking a moment, and at none it wakes up aboard the boat with nothing lost. Hearts come back one every 6 s out of the fight, and all of them aboard. The Throw button prefers a monkey to a fish; two hits beat a monkey (one with the barbed spear), and a beaten one drops its skull mask (kept, and counted: masks in the save, a chip and a guide page) and runs off, back a minute later. Beat all five and the chest opens with 400 coins; the camp fills again 90 s later. In the tests a person with the first spear who backs off from the nearest monkey and throws whenever ready beats the camp without being bonked out at least fifteen times in twenty.
- The tower and its boss (2026-10-07), the kid's mini dungeon: island 2's stone tower is climbed from the inside, real time and never turn-based. Walk to the door on its front face and a green Climb button appears; inside are two stone floors lit by torches, three monkeys on the first and four on the second (two of them throwing coconuts), and the glowing stairs at the back of each floor open once it is beaten. Climbing the stairs refills the hearts. On the roof, under the sky, waits the leviathan sorcerer, the kid's naga-ish chimera: a finned serpent tail, a feathered purple body, two wings and a bird's head with a hooked beak. It has 8 health, flies a ring round the roof throwing slow purple bolts at where the figure stands (so moving is the counter), and every 5 s swoops in to peck, which is when a spear reaches it; a hit on the swoop drives it back. At half health it gets angry: faster, red-eyed, three bolts at a time. A bonk, a coconut, a bolt or a peck each take a heart, and at none the figure wakes aboard with nothing lost, as in the camp. Beaten, it falls in a burst of feathers: 2,000 coins the first time and 500 every time after, the tower is taken (towerTaken), your flag flies from its top for good, and a Back to the boat button takes the figure straight to its boat, the kid's teleport. A Leave button walks out of the tower from any floor. Hearts do not come back while the camp or the sorcerer is fighting. In the tests a person who strafes round the roof and throws whenever the sorcerer is in reach wins 13 in 30 with the first spear, 27 with the long spear and 30 with the barbed spear, so the tower asks for an upgrade or two without walling the first spear off. The floors are rooms in a pocket of the world far from any sea (world/tower.ts), so the walker, the monkeys, the spears and the camera serve unchanged; that is a change from the proposal of stairs up the outside, which would have needed the camera to follow height. The sorcerer's shapes are a stand-in: he is the kid's to draw.
- Plants (2026-10-08), for the kid who wants the game to look richer and more real: both islands now grow flowering bushes (hibiscus red, frangipani cream, bougainvillea pink, plumeria yellow, lavender), patches of small five-petalled flowers in the grass, and tufts of beach grass round the sand and on the beach, and under the water there are meadows of sea grass in the shallows and swaying kelp further out. The sea grass and kelp draw under the waves and the fish, the flowers and tufts flat with the island, and the bushes with the solids. Everything sways a little, the kelp slowest. The owner's steer on "more realistic" was noted the night before: richer rather than photographic, since the flat cosy look is what the renderer does well; this is the first step of that.

## How the prototype is built

One IIFE, plain canvas 2D, DOM for HUD and shop, Web Audio oscillators for sound, Grandstander from Google Fonts.

**Projection.** `K = √½`. Screen x = `((wx−camX) − (wy−camY))·K·Z + W/2`. Screen y = `((wx−camX) + (wy−camY))·K/2·Z + H/2 + viewDY − wz·Z`. A world circle of radius r draws as an ellipse `r·Z` by `r·Z/2`. Joystick screen vectors are inverted back to world with `a = dx/K, b = 2dy/K → ((a+b)/2, (b−a)/2)`. `Z` is a zoom derived from `min(W,H)/500`, clamped 0.62–1.35, eased by tier and by the dock view.

**Solids.** `extrude(poly, z0, z1, side, top)` draws only viewer-facing side quads (normal·(1,1) > 0), shaded by normal, then the top face. Ships are hull polygon + inset deck + cabin boxes rotated by heading, so they foreshorten correctly. Depth sorting is by `wx + wy` for a short list of drawables (hut, palms, tree, tower, boat, pirate). The pier is a special case that sorts relative to the boat's side of it.

**Update order.** clock → input → boat physics → island/pier push-out → world clamp → range limit → net tow → schools + catching → dock + selling → rare → leviathan → sparks → pirate → gulls + dolphins → sharks → salvage → driftwood → zoom → wakes/particles/texts → camera.

**Draw order.** sea (abyss, lagoon diamond, depth rings, glints, range ring, island flats, dock ring) → leviathan → fish → rare → sharks → dolphins → wakes → net → buoys → salvage → driftwood → depth-sorted objects → birds → flying-fish particles → night mask → glow pass (bioluminescence, sparkles) → floating text → edge indicators → joystick.

**Night.** A half-resolution offscreen canvas is filled with the ambient colour, lights are punched out with `destination-out` radial gradients scaled by `dark`, and the result is multiplied onto the scene. Glow species then get a `screen` halo and per-fish bright dots. This replaced an additive glow that washed the boat out; do not go back to adding light on top of sprites.

**Steering around land.** `around(x, y, tx, ty, R)` returns a tangent waypoint when the straight line to a target would cross the island. Dolphins and the pirate use it, with `pushOut` as a backstop. Any new roaming entity needs the same.

**Debug hook.** `window.__np` exposes boat, net, schools, pirate, sharks, pods, rare, lev, and setters for clock, wood and build; `hold` and `coins` are getters, `wood` is a setter only, so read the wood count from the HUD pill `#wood`. The smoke test drives the game through it, and so do balance probes in headless Chromium (max the levels, set `build` and `wood`, drop a log on the boat, read the shop). Keep an equivalent in dev builds.

## Known debt (fix during the port, not before)

- Global mutable state in one closure. No entity interface, no layers, no event bus.
- Species are coupled by array index (`SHARK = 7`, night = 8–9, rares = 10–11, `RING_R` parallel array). Move to string ids and data files; migrate the save's `log[]` to a map.
- `refreshShop()` rebuilds paint swatches and log chips via `innerHTML` on every sale tick.
- Toasts (fixed 2026-09-17): `ui/toast.ts` is a queue with three priorities; danger interrupts, the rest wait up to four seconds and give up, and a message never repeats back to back. Pirate and shark warnings are danger; dolphins, tier, palace, the leviathan and the range edge are events; everything else is routine.
- Pier depth-sort hack. Tall palace pieces can overlap the hut at some camera positions.
- Fish heading is derived from frame-to-frame position delta; schools that come on screen snap for a frame.
- Leviathan path speed is non-uniform (squircle parameterisation).
- No pause beyond clamping dt to 50 ms. The trip survives a discarded tab (see Persistence); entities do not, so schools, sharks and the pirate reset on reload.
- Sound is raw oscillators by design. `audio/sfx.ts` (2026-09-17) names the twenty-five cues and locks each to the legacy numbers by test; the catch, sell, driftwood blip and dolphin whistle are keepers (owner and kid, 2026-09-16 and 17). Music and ambience are separate layers and never replace a cue.
- Dead code: `isDark`, `mq` (night used to follow the OS theme).
- The palace is small. It reads as a treehouse wearing a hat. It deserves its own art pass.
- Accessibility: canvas has a label and the shop is real buttons, but there is no reduced-motion path for screen shake or the day-cycle tint.

So any press is a full pull, and the reel pulls at 48 (was 34), half again as fast as a run, so a sulk wins back more than a run takes. In the real game, a thumb dragging 15 px a third of a second late, doing what the words said, lost five landable fish of five to the piling before and landed five of five after. The giant runs at 36 (was 34) so that it stays the one that gets away; this entry used to promise five in six. ## Look and voice

- Palette anchors: sea `#2B8A99` / `#1B6676`, foam `#F3FFFB`, sand `#F2D79B`, buoy red `#E4572E`, cream `#FFF6E5`, coin `#FFC53D`, ink `#12303A`. Flat shaded, no outlines, light from the left (+y faces lighter than +x faces).
- Type: Grandstander 500/700/800, with a rounded system fallback. HUD chrome is pills; the shop is a plank sign.
- Copy: sentence case, plain verbs, a button says what it does ("Buy for 20", "Build the treehouse"), the confirmation uses the same words ("Built the treehouse"). Errors say what to do next ("Collect 5 more driftwood out at sea."). No exclamation marks except a rare catch.

## Things learned the hard way

- A canvas with `position:fixed; inset:0` does not stretch. Set `width:100%; height:100%` or the backing store draws at the wrong scale.
- Additive glow over sprites at night washes them out and makes them look darker. Use a darkness mask with holes.
- Panning the camera the moment the boat crosses the dock ring feels like being yanked. Wait until the boat has slowed, zoom out, drift gently.
- Anything that roams needs to know the island exists.
- In night scenes, anything the player must find (driftwood, crates, rares) needs its own small light.
- Test at phone size first. The isometric squash halves vertical distances, so the world feels smaller on screen than the numbers suggest.
- Vite's default ports are shared by every Vite project on the machine, and Playwright's `reuseExistingServer` will happily test whichever app answers first. Pin ports and never reuse.
- Vite blocks any Host header that is not localhost or an IP address. A dev server reached by hostname needs `server.allowedHosts`.
- A network that advertises IPv6 without routing it hangs Node downloads, including Playwright's browser installer, while curl falls back to IPv4 in milliseconds. If curl works and Node does not, suspect IPv6 first.
- Git Bash's tar cannot read zip files. `C:\Windows\System32\tar.exe` can.
- A force-push does not start a GitHub Pages branch build, and with the Pages source set to GitHub Actions nothing deploys until a workflow exists on `main`.
- Phone browsers discard a backgrounded tab. Anything not saved is a lost trip.
- A panel that covers the thumb's home must let steering through. Pointer capture makes it one binding: a press on the panel's background anchors the stick and hands the pointer to the canvas.
- iOS ignores `user-scalable=no`, so a double-tap on any button zooms the page, and once zoomed the canvas's `touch-action: none` swallows the pinch that would zoom back out. `touch-action: manipulation` on the body kills double-tap zoom everywhere and leaves pinch alone. Found by the chief playtester buying an upgrade twice, fast.
- Coins come from the sweep and wood from errands, so a base gated only on wood always trails the boat. Fix supply before price, and give the late stages something to gate.
- Price nothing off a single big docking. Take two screenshots ten minutes apart of the owner actually playing and divide; that number was a tenth of the burst.
- A timing test played by a hand that reacts on the same frame proves nothing about people. Give the test hand a person's reaction, a third of a second, and the game's own input smoothing. The snook's fight passed a perfect hand and parted every line a person played.
- Then give it a person's input too. A thumb on glass is down or up, and a short drag is how people hold. The snook's second fix passed a test hand that dragged three quarters of the ring and eased to a fifth through every run; the owner, holding the way people hold, lost every fish to the piling.
- Trigger a threat on what the player did, not where they are pointing. The leviathan's guard first rose for any flagship heading at the buoys, and the lanternfish school 280 in from them: it would have woken on every sweep of the richest fish in the game.
- Players reach for what the world shows them. The day they could walk past the dog, both players went looking for a way to pet it. Make the obvious touch work, and show how until it is found.
- On the far side of an isometric island a figure stands in front of the water behind it, so one walked to the waterline looks as if it is wading. Keep feet a shadow's width inside the sand.
- Eight-way keys on an isometric map point at a diagonal the boat is rarely facing, so the throttle penalty for turning is on almost all the time and the keyboard feels slower and twitchier than the stick. Put feels side by side behind a switch and let the players pick. Two players picked two different ones, and a setting was cheaper than a winner.
